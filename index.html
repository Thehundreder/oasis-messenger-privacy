Privacy Policy — Oasis Chat Assistant

Applies to: version 0.4.0
Last updated: 9 November 2026
Publisher: Smail Moulouad
Contact: ismail.moulouad@gmail.com

This policy describes how version 0.4.0 of the Oasis Chat Assistant browser extension ("the extension") handles information, and lists every server the extension itself contacts.

The short version

  • The publisher receives none of your data. The extension runs in your own Chrome browser. The publisher runs no server for it and receives none of your data, your contacts' data, or usage statistics from it. There is no analytics, advertising, tracking, or crash reporting in the extension. (Google may show the publisher aggregate install counts on the Chrome Web Store, and the publisher receives any email you send for support.)
  • It reads the open Messenger or Instagram conversation automatically, inside your browser. Nothing leaves your device until you click Generate suggested reply, Detect from conversation or Ask.
  • The panel asks you to confirm before anything is sent. Until you confirm, the Chat tab shows a notice explaining what gets sent. Generate suggested reply, Detect from conversation and Ask send nothing until you click "I understand — continue"; after that you click them again. A link to this policy sits in the notice, under the Generate button and in Settings.
  • The Ask tab answers your own questions about the company's products, ingredients and fees. Your question goes to OpenRouter with the matching company information. The open conversation goes with it only if you tick "Include this chat in my question" for that question. To find the company information, Ask reads public Oriflame web pages and its product catalog, and downloads a public copy of the company knowledge base. It does this without your Oriflame login, and only after you allow those sites in a Chrome prompt.
  • When you click Generate, information is sent to OpenRouter, a separate AI service you sign up to yourself and pay per use, using your own API key. OpenRouter passes it on to the company that runs the AI model. That includes the messages of the open conversation (both people's), the other person's name, and everything you have saved for replies: your notes about the person, your personal note and your talking points. Email addresses and phone numbers in the conversation messages are replaced first, but that is incomplete and covers only the messages.
  • Generate also looks for products the other person mentions. It sends her last four messages (redacted the same way) to OpenRouter in a separate short request, with the list of the company's brand names, so it can recognise a product even when it is misspelt. Only the codes of the products found are then checked on Oriflame's public product catalog (api.oriflame.com). The product list itself ships inside the extension.
  • The extension never sends a message for you. Clicking a suggestion puts it into the open chat's message box, replacing anything already there. You press Send yourself.
  • What you type into the extension is saved only in your browser, on your device. Your API key is stored encrypted; the rest is stored without encryption.

What the extension is for

The extension is for people who use Messenger or Instagram to talk with prospects and team members about a business opportunity (for example, direct-selling brand partners). It reads the conversation you have open and asks an AI model to draft suggested replies, including first messages to people you have not spoken to before. It drafts every reply as if the person is a prospect (someone you might invite to join, the only kind of contact the extension supports), and tailors the drafts with your notes about them and a generation label (such as "Gen Z"). The AI also returns a short read of how receptive the person seems, which is shown in the panel and not saved. The Ask tab answers your own questions about the company's products, ingredients and fees, so you can answer the people you talk to correctly. Each answer shows the company sources it came from. You review, edit and send every message yourself. Helping you with these conversations is the extension's only purpose.

What the extension reads on the page

Where it runs. The extension is loaded when you open www.messenger.com or a www.facebook.com/messages page. It does not run on other websites and does not read your other browser tabs. Because Facebook changes pages without reloading them, the extension can stay loaded if you move to another part of Facebook in the same tab; it checks the page address first and, unless the tab is on Messenger or www.facebook.com/messages, reads no page text and types nothing (it still notices that the page changed, and does nothing with that).

On Instagram, the extension is loaded when you open a www.instagram.com/direct page. There it reads only a conversation you have open (www.instagram.com/direct/t/…), never your inbox list, feed, profiles or stories. If you open your messages from another part of Instagram without the page reloading, the extension asks you to reload the page first.

What it reads. Automatically, even when the side panel is closed, it reads:

  • the text of the messages Messenger has loaded in the open conversation (not only the part on screen), with no limit on how many, from every participant (in a group chat, everyone), and whether each message was written by you, worked out from where it appears on screen or from Messenger's hidden screen-reader text;
  • any other text shown in the conversation area, such as link-preview text, the text of links, and some date, time and status labels (the extension tries to skip these, but its filter misses some, especially if Messenger is not set to English);
  • a best guess at the other person's name from the top of the conversation. It fills in the "Contact's name" field automatically. You can change it; if the extension can find a name on the page, it fills an empty field in again.

It does not read photos or the web addresses behind links.

On Instagram it reads the text of the messages Instagram has loaded in the open conversation, from every participant, and whether each message was written by you, worked out from Instagram's hidden button labels or from where it appears on screen. Instagram only keeps nearby messages on the page, so the extension also keeps what it has already read of this conversation in that tab's memory, until you open another conversation or reload the page. It also reads the other person's display name from the top of the conversation, and their username, which is used only inside the page to tell their messages from yours and is not sent anywhere. When they share a post, reel or story, only the fact that they shared one is kept, not the account it came from. If Instagram does not show recognisable messages, the extension reads nothing rather than other text on the screen.

When Messenger does not show recognisable messages (for example in a brand-new conversation, or after Messenger changes its layout), the extension can read other text on the screen instead, such as the names and message previews of your other chats in the left-hand list. If you then click Generate, that text is sent too. The Chat tab shows what was read, so check it before you click Generate.

Scrolling. When you click Generate, or ask in the Ask tab with the chat included, the extension scrolls the conversation to the top twice, for up to about three seconds in all, so Messenger loads earlier messages (that request is made by Messenger's own page), then scrolls back. On Instagram it moves up one screen at a time, twice, for up to about three seconds in all, then moves back.

Typing into Messenger. When you click a suggestion, the extension deletes anything already in Messenger's message box (including a draft you had started) and types the suggestion in gradually, a character or two at a time at about the pace of a person typing, so a short reply takes a few seconds. Messenger may show the other person a "typing" indicator while this happens; that indicator comes from Messenger, not from the extension. The extension never presses Send, and it does not tell the other person that a reply was drafted with AI help; whether to tell them is up to you. For up to five minutes afterwards it checks, inside your browser, whether the message box has become empty (which usually means you sent or cleared it) to keep the counter on the toolbar icon. It does not store or send what is in the box.

Typing into Instagram. When you click a suggestion on Instagram, the extension clears the message box (including a draft you had started) and types the suggestion in gradually, at the same pace as on Messenger, with any line breaks replaced by spaces. If it can't clear the box, it tells you. Instagram may show the other person a "typing" indicator; that would come from Instagram, not from the extension. The rest of the paragraph above applies in the same way: the extension never presses Send, and whether to say a reply was drafted with AI help is up to you.

Information you give the extension

All of this is optional except your API key:

  • your OpenRouter API key;
  • your market (United Kingdom or Romania), which sets the panel language and the product catalog used;
  • for each conversation: free-text notes about the person you are speaking with, how you know them (including an optional free-text answer), their age if you type it, and the contact's name (see above);
  • reply language, emoji and short-message settings, and an optional "pivot to a new topic" note;
  • a short personal note and talking points you want reflected in replies;
  • an optional product code.

What leaves your device, and where it goes

1. OpenRouter (when you click Generate or Detect from conversation)

The extension sends a request over HTTPS from your browser to openrouter.ai, authenticated with your own API key. OpenRouter forwards it to the company that runs the AI model (see "OpenRouter" below).

Generate suggested reply. The request contains:

  • the messages read from the conversation, after the redaction described below;
  • the other person's name, your notes about them, how you know them, and a generation label if one is set (your typed age is not sent, only the label);
  • your language, market, emoji and short-message settings, and any pivot note;
  • your personal note and talking points;
  • product details and the product code, if you entered one (see section 2);
  • the names of up to six products from one public product page of your market's site, if the other person mentions a need such as dry skin and you have allowed the company sites in the Ask tab (see section 2);
  • the names and short descriptions of real products the other person seems to mention, taken from the product list inside the extension, if product search found any (see "Product search" below);
  • the extension's built-in instructions and reference material, which contain no information about you or your contacts.

If you have not entered an age, the extension also scans the other person's messages for a stated age or generation (for example "I'm 34" or "millennial"). If it finds one, it adds the matching generation label to the request and saves that label for the conversation, without asking you.

To get a reply, the extension tries up to three different AI models in turn, moving to the next if one fails or times out and retrying a model once if it reports being busy. A single click can therefore send the same request up to six times. Each attempt sends the same information to the same address.

Product search (added 2026-10-28). When you click Generate, and before the request above, the extension sends one more, separate short request to OpenRouter so it can recognise products the other person mentions, even misspelt or written in Romanian (for example "posses" for Possess). That request contains:

  • the last four messages written by the other person (in a group chat, by the others), not yours, after the redaction described below;
  • the list of the company's brand names for your market (about 50), with a few example product names for each, taken from the product list inside the extension;
  • the extension's built-in instructions for this step.

It is tried at most twice and stops after 8 seconds. If it fails, the extension looks for brand names in those messages on your device instead, without AI. The result is kept in the panel's memory, so clicking Generate again on the same conversation does not send this request again. If the other person has written nothing, or the product list for your market is missing, no such request is made. What the AI answers is only used to pick products from the product list inside the extension; their codes are then checked as described in section 2.

Detect from conversation (Contact tab). The request contains your API key and the last six messages the extension has read, from both people, and is tried on up to three models in turn. This request does not apply the redaction below.

Redaction. Before a Generate request (the product-search request included), and before an Ask request that includes the chat, the extension replaces anything in the conversation messages that looks like an email address, or a phone-number-like run of seven or more digits (including dates, prices and order numbers written that way), with a placeholder such as "[redacted email]". This is a best-effort safeguard and is not complete. It does not remove names, street addresses, social-media handles, web links, numbers shorter than seven digits, or contact details that are spelled out or disguised, and it does not remove sensitive details a person writes in their own words. It also does not apply to anything you enter in the extension's own fields (notes, the "Other" answer to how you know someone, personal note, talking points, pivot note, product code, contact name). Do not put sensitive information in those fields. When a Generate request succeeds and the redaction changed something, the panel shows a notice above the suggestions; if the request fails, no notice is shown.

OpenRouter. OpenRouter is a separate company and handles requests under its own terms, not this policy; see https://openrouter.ai/privacy and https://openrouter.ai/terms. OpenRouter does not run the AI models itself: it passes each request on to the company that runs the chosen model (for example Microsoft Azure for OpenAI's model, Google, or a company hosting DeepSeek's model). The extension asks OpenRouter to use only endpoints that do not keep requests after answering (zero data retention) and do not train AI on them; the publisher relies on OpenRouter's description of those policies and cannot check them. OpenRouter also receives your IP address and the usual technical details of a web request, and can link requests to your OpenRouter account through your key. Each request carries a random identifier, created when the panel opens and never stored, so that OpenRouter sends requests to the same company and part of the prompt can be reused. It says nothing about you or the other person. The publisher does not control OpenRouter or those companies, has no access to anything you send, and cannot confirm where they process data.

2. Oriflame's product catalog and product pages (when you click Generate)

A product code you enter. If you type a product code into the optional product field and click Generate, the extension requests that product's details from Oriflame's public product catalog at api.oriflame.com (until 28 October 2026 this used a test version of the catalog). The request is an ordinary web request containing only the product code and a locale derived from your market setting (en-GB or ro-RO). It sends no cookies, does not say which page you came from, and contains no conversation text, notes, login or API key. A second request of the same kind then fetches the product's general details (name, description) by the product-family code the first answer gives, so a code for one shade or size also gets its product name. If the product is discontinued or out of stock, the response may name a replacement product, and the extension then requests up to two further products. That address receives your IP address and the usual technical details of a web request and handles them under its operator's own practices, not this policy. What it returns (name, availability, description, ingredients, benefits, how to use), or a note that the lookup failed, is included in the request to OpenRouter together with the product code. If you leave the field empty, no such request is made.

Products found by product search (added 2026-10-28). When product search (section 1) picks products from the product list inside the extension, the extension checks whether each one is still sold, with the same kind of request to api.oriflame.com: only the product code and the locale, no cookies, no referrer, no conversation text. At most three products are checked for each product the other person mentions. A product the catalog reports as discontinued is not suggested; if the check fails, or the catalog reports the product out of stock, the AI is told not to say the product is in stock.

The product list inside the extension. The list product search uses (product names, short public descriptions, categories, shades and sizes, and whether each is still sold; no prices) was built by the publisher from Oriflame's public product pages and ships inside the extension package. It is not downloaded while you use the extension and contains no information about you.

Products for a need the other person mentions (added 2026-10-27). If you have allowed the company sites in the Ask tab (section 3) and the other person's last four messages mention a need the extension recognises (for example dry skin), clicking Generate also reads one or two public product-category pages on your market's site (uk.oriflame.com or ro.oriflame.com), chosen from the site's own page list. The request is made the same way as Ask's: without your Oriflame login or cookies and without a referrer. The page address shows the need (for example a "hydrating" category); no conversation text is sent to the site. The names of up to six products listed on that page are included in the request to OpenRouter. If you have not allowed the sites, or no need is recognised, no such request is made.

3. The Ask tab (only when you use it)

Allowing the sites. The first time you use Ask, the panel shows an Allow access button. Clicking it opens Chrome's own prompt for the public Oriflame site of your market (uk.oriflame.com or ro.oriflame.com) and the address of the knowledge-base copy (metabloomglobal.blob.core.windows.net). Until you allow them, Ask sends nothing anywhere. You can withdraw this at any time on the extension's page in Chrome (chrome://extensions).

Knowledge base. Ask downloads a public copy of the company knowledge base (questions and answers about the business) from metabloomglobal.blob.core.windows.net, a Microsoft Azure storage address that the publisher controls. Like any web server, it receives your IP address and the usual technical details of the request. It downloads only your market's language and English, at most once every six hours while the panel is open, and keeps the copy on your device (see "What is stored"). The request carries no information about you beyond the usual technical details of a web request.

When you click Ask, the extension:

  • picks the matching parts of the knowledge base and of the reference material that ships inside the extension, on your device;
  • if your question contains product codes, requests those products' details (up to three, plus any official replacement) from Oriflame's public product catalog at api.oriflame.com. The request contains only the product code and your market's language;
  • if needed, reads up to a few public pages of your market's Oriflame site (for example the membership and fee pages, or the shop pages for a need). Only pages the extension lists for your market can be read. These requests are made without your Oriflame login (no cookies) and without telling the site which page you came from;
  • sends a request to OpenRouter (openrouter.ai) with your API key. It contains your question (up to 1,000 characters); your previous three questions and the extension's answers in this panel session (each answer shortened to 600 characters); the company information picked above; the conversation you have open, but only if you ticked "Include this chat in my question" for that question (up to its last 8,000 characters, after the redaction described in section 1); and the extension's built-in instructions. One click makes at most two such requests, tried on up to three AI models with at most six attempts in total. The redaction does not apply to the question you type, so do not type personal details into a question. When the redaction changed something in the chat, the answer shows a notice.

Ask never sends your notes, personal note or talking points.

4. Nothing else

In this version, the only servers the extension itself contacts are the ones above. It sends nothing to the publisher. The product list used by product search ships inside the extension and is not downloaded. Messenger's or Instagram's own page makes its own requests as you use it (for example loading earlier messages when the extension scrolls, and handling text placed in the message box). All of the extension's code ships inside the extension package; it does not download or run code from the internet.

How information is used

This extension's handling of user data adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Information is used only to provide the extension's single purpose: helping you with your Messenger and Instagram conversations, by drafting the reply suggestions you ask for and answering your questions about the company's products. It is sent to OpenRouter (and, for product codes only, to the catalog address in sections 2 and 3) only because that is necessary for that purpose. It is not sold or transferred to advertising platforms, data brokers or other information resellers; it is not used for personalised or interest-based advertising or to determine creditworthiness or for lending; and the publisher does not read it. It may be disclosed only where required by law or to protect against fraud or abuse.

Deleting your information

  • Notes, age and "how you know them" for a conversation are updated as you edit them. Clearing them removes their content but leaves an empty record labelled with that conversation's web address, and the extension has no button to delete those records. The generation label is reset by typing an age and clearing it again.
  • Earlier versions saved a contact category (prospect, recruit, starter, leader or inactive) for each conversation. Version 0.3.0 no longer asks for one and deletes those saved categories the first time its side panel opens.
  • To change or remove your API key, personal note or talking points, edit or clear the field and click its Save button. Chrome may keep deleted values in its files for a while before overwriting them, and version 0.2.1 or earlier stored the API key unencrypted, so revoke a key in your OpenRouter account if you want to be sure an old copy can't be used.
  • Removing the extension from Chrome normally also deletes everything in the table above; this is done by the browser, not by the extension. It does not affect information already sent to OpenRouter or the product catalog address, text you copied to your clipboard, or text left in a chat's message box.
  • The publisher holds no copy of your information, so there is nothing for the publisher to delete. For anything held by OpenRouter, contact OpenRouter.

Your rights

Everything the extension stores is on your device, where you can see, correct and delete it as described above. If you email the publisher for support, the publisher uses your email address and message only to reply to you and keeps them only as long as needed for that. You can also complain to your national data protection authority.

The people you talk to

The extension processes whatever appears in the conversations you choose, including messages written by other people, who have not agreed to anything and cannot see this policy. That can include sensitive details they write about themselves (for example about health or beliefs); these are sent to OpenRouter as part of the conversation and are not removed by the redaction. You decide which conversations to use the extension on. Use it only where you are comfortable sending that conversation to an AI service, make sure that is appropriate under the law and the terms of the services involved, and avoid typing sensitive details about people into the notes fields. The extension does not check anyone's age; do not use it for conversations with anyone under 18.

Who the extension is for

The extension is intended for adults. It is not directed at children.

Security

Requests to OpenRouter, the product catalog addresses, the Oriflame pages and the knowledge-base copy use HTTPS. Content downloaded for Ask is treated as text and is never run as code. Your API key is stored encrypted, so it does not appear in readable form in the extension's settings storage. The encryption key is kept in the same Chrome profile, though, so someone with a full copy of your browser profile (or a backup of it), or malicious software running on your computer, could still recover your API key. Your notes are kept in Chrome's extension storage without extra encryption, so anyone with access to your computer and browser profile may be able to read them. If you think your API key has been exposed, revoke it and create a new one in your OpenRouter account; that is the only way to make an old copy useless.

Changes to this policy

If the extension's handling of information changes, this page will be updated and the "Last updated" date above will change. Changes that send information to a new destination will also be described on the extension's Chrome Web Store listing, and the panel will show its notice again for you to confirm.
